The paradox is clean. Two of your top three posts are behind-the-scenes content, and the top post outperforms the next-best post by a factor of three. Yet the bulk of the feed continues to lead with product photography and inspirational stylings, which average 482 likes. Inspirational content is not failing in any absolute sense, it is just not your highest-leverage format. The same content production effort, redirected toward more recipe walkthroughs, kitchen-side process moments, ingredient sourcing trips, and baker-led explainers, would compound the feed's reach without any change in posting cadence.

One operational note. The funnel mix on your feed is 84% TOFU (top of funnel, awareness-led). Only 14% sits at the MOFU stage where consideration and education live. Behind-the-scenes content is almost always MOFU-coded by the algorithm, which is why it gets disproportionate reach: viewers stay longer, save more, and the algorithm reads that as quality signal. The platform is already telling you what to do. The feed mix is what's not listening yet.

Beat 1: the category is at full volume

Forty-one brands are actively running paid social in this category right now, between them generating 797 unique creatives over the analysis window. The brands with the most active creatives are Pieminister (47), Brigit's Bakery (24), Volcano Coffee Works (18), Origin Coffee Roasters (16) and Gaya Bakery (13). These are the "testing factories," brands running large creative volumes to find what works. None of them are direct GAIL's competitors. The actual bakery and café chains in the category are running smaller portfolios.

Beat 2: most ads die quickly

Of the 797 creatives, 381 (48%) ran for fewer than 30 days. Only 38 creatives have survived past 60 days and remain live. That's a 4.8% survival rate, which is notably worse than the broader Meta ad-platform benchmark of around 11%. Your category is harder to crack than average. The ads that have made it past 60 days are the only ones worth studying, because every other ad in the dataset is a creative bet that the platform has already rejected. The 60-day still-active pool is the category's verified-winning set.

Active ads by brand vs median runtime (top 12)

Beat 3: the winners look very different from the also-rans

The 38 ads that have survived the platform's profitability filter share a clear DNA. Compared to the full 797-ad dataset, the winners are 25 percentage points more likely to be video, 14 points more likely to be creator-led UGC, and 30 points more likely to be classified as Inspirational rather than Promotional content. The dying format is the static-graphic single-image carousel running a Promotional message. The surviving format is medium-production-complexity video with an Inspirational frame.

Two single-brand outliers carry most of the category's verified-winning learning. Origin Coffee Roasters owns six of the 38 winners, including a 511-day still-running UGC video built around a "Work From Home with our coffee" hook (the longest-running creator-led ad in the category by a wide margin). The Chai Guys own seven of the winners, mostly product-only carousels in a saturated red colour palette that anchor a recognisable brand pattern across the funnel. Neither brand is a direct GAIL's competitor, but their ads are the closest thing the category has to a proven playbook.

Beat 4: where GAIL's actually sits on the map

GAIL's runs one active paid creative. It is a five-day-old cinematic video promoting the Spring Menu, classified as TOFU with a Polished Brand style. You have the foundations. You don't have the programme.

That number is unusual for a brand of GAIL's size, and it reflects the genuine cultural foothold the chain has in London. The problem is not awareness. The problem is that almost none of these 60 creators are the creators who actually drive reach in the category, and the activity that does exist is concentrated inside a handful of small, working partnerships rather than a continuous programme.

The conversation gap, by the numbers

Of your 60 organic creators in 90 days, only three carry an explicit paid-partnership signal. That number sounds like a strength (organic reach!) but it is more accurately a tell. It means the brand has not been investing in scaling these relationships. The top organic post in the window pulled 11,995 likes from Tanya Burr; the second pulled 2,193 from Ben's Dose of Life; the third pulled 1,449 from Marbie's Kitchen. Below the top three, engagement drops sharply. Most of the 60 creators are nano and micro accounts where a single GAIL's tag is a one-off, not the start of a relationship.

Meanwhile, in the wider category, the most-followed and most-active creators are concentrated around Blank Street, Bread Ahead, and Maison Ladurée. They are creators choosing to make Blank Street the subject of their content because Blank Street has spent two years building product moments that creators want to post.

A note on TikTok. GAIL's posts to TikTok at roughly the same cadence as Instagram (37 posts in 90 days), but average views per post sit at 3,442 with average likes of 102. 06 The Opportunity

Six moves that change the game for GAIL's.

Each of these moves is grounded in something we found in the data above. None of them require new positioning, new product, or new brand. They require turning an underbuilt programme into a built one, and matching the chain's operational pace with an equivalent creator pace.

Opportunity 01

Scale what you already run.

You have a book club, a run-club programme, a preview-event circuit, and a mum-network presence. None of them are at chain scale. Take each programme, define a city-level template, and replicate it across the 40 stores opened in the last year. One book-club host per region. Two run-club partners per region. A mum-network ambassador per region. Same brief, locally executed.

40 new stores. 4 cities with creator activity.
Opportunity 02

Activate the new locations.

The south-west cohort (Bristol, Bath, Salisbury, Marlborough) opened with no recorded local-creator activations. Harrogate opens in the second half of 2026. Source 5 to 10 local food and lifestyle creators per opening, six to eight weeks ahead of launch, with a brief built around a soft preview and a launch-week post. Each opening is its own micro-campaign.

5 new cities. 1 confirmed upcoming.
Opportunity 03

Triple the behind-the-scenes ratio.

Your top-performing pillar is behind-the-scenes content and you post it eight times per 50. The carrot cake recipe Reel pulled 9,924 likes, four times your next-best post. Move BTS from 16% of feed to roughly 40%. Recipes, ingredient sourcing, baker explainers, kitchen process. Each piece becomes content GAIL's owns, and creator-source briefs to commission too.

BTS is 3x engagement. 16% of output.
Opportunity 04

Build a continuous paid creator pipeline.

The category runs 797 ads and 38 of them have survived 60+ days. You run one ad. Start a continuous paid pipeline: 6 to 10 creator-led video assets in market at all times, 60-day testing windows, winners extended. The category's verified-winning DNA is medium-production-complexity video with an Inspirational frame, classified UGC or Polished Brand. Match that DNA.

797 ads in category. You run one.
Opportunity 05

Test Origin Coffee's 511-day pattern.

The longest-running creator-led ad in your category is Origin Coffee's "Work From Home with our coffee" UGC video, 511 days and still in market. The closest GAIL's analogue is "Weekday morning ritual" or "WFH from a GAIL's table" with 3 to 5 mid-tier creators filming UGC clips of the morning order, the laptop setup, the cinnamon bun. Single-pattern bet, market-verified for 17 months.

511 days. One angle. Already proven.
Opportunity 06

Take the lifestyle creators back from Blank Street.

13 of the top 30 IG creators in this category have posted about Blank Street. 18 of the top 30 TT creators have. Zero have posted about GAIL's. These are the same creators who post fashion, motherhood, run-club, and London-life content, the exact themes your existing organic creators already use GAIL's for. Source 20 to 30 of them in Q3 with a summer iced-drink or seasonal-menu hook.

13 of 30 tag Blank Street. 0 tag GAIL's.
