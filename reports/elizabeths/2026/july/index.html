Elizabeth's has 19 own-feed posts to read (Instagram and TikTok posts the brand published itself), all inside one 84-day burst from 19 Feb to 14 May 2026. Fifteen are video, three are photos, and one is a carousel. They average 15 likes and a middle value of 8, small numbers off a 552-follower base. Underneath those averages, though, is a much sharper signal about which kind of post the audience actually rewards.

Sorted by likes per post, the order almost inverts the production schedule. The single Behind-the-Scenes post, the family hand-building the booth, pulled 87 likes. The Relatable/Meme posts (the crouton-emoji petition among them) averaged 21, Promotional posts 12, Entertaining 10, and Inspirational 6. Promotional is also the pillar the brand makes most, 10 of 19 posts, more than half the feed, at the second-lowest average on the board. The thing that works best is made least, and the thing made most works nearly worst.

Format-wise, video carries the feed: 15 of the 19 posts, averaging 15 likes. The one carousel, the finished-booth reveal, drew the most comments of any post despite a low hook score, and the three photos trail at 6. The way the brand shoots leans on-camera and hand-made, brand-rep UGC from its own team is the biggest archetype at 36.8%, ahead of polished studio work at 21.1% and animation at 15.8%, which is why the most human post outperforming the most polished one is not an accident.

The multiplier is the whole story: 87 likes on the one Behind-the-Scenes post against a 12-like Promotional average is a 7.2x gap, from a single data point but a very loud one. The instruction to the content team is simple: make more of the family, the bakery and the process, and fewer flat product shots.

The Biggest Miss

Your one Behind-the-Scenes post, the family building the booth, pulled 87 likes, 7.2x the 12-like average of the Promotional posts that make up more than half your feed. You made one Behind-the-Scenes post and ten Promotional ones. Flip that ratio and the family, not the packshot, becomes the reason people follow a five-generation bakery.
