Rizz sits in the bottom slice of the niche on every creator metric: rank 120 of 128 by Instagram followers, and rank 99 and 103 of 128 by Creator Tagged Posts (Instagram and TikTok posts where a creator tagged the brand inside the window) and Total Creator Views. The four brands beside it, Lemme, Olly, Goli and Grüns — Rizz's closest twin, greens in a gummy bear — each carry six-figure followings and dozens to hundreds of creator posts. The social footprint and the creator conversation are not close to lined up yet.

Where Rizz sits

The gap is not that Rizz is quiet, it posts about 7 times a week, but that none of that output is being carried by anyone other than Rizz itself, which is exactly what the next section looks at.

We kept looking for the creator conversation around Rizz and could not find one inside the 69-day window. There were no Creator Tagged Posts on Instagram or TikTok in that period. What does exist is just outside it: six unpaid Instagram posts and one TikTok between late October 2025 and 3 March 2026, including a one-gummy-a-day testimonial from @alexxmara that pulled 59.7K views. The earned-mention engine has clearly fired before; it has simply gone quiet.

That makes the read on paid versus organic unusual. With zero paid creator posts and zero organic ones in the window, this is a earned-mention engine sitting idle rather than a programme being run badly. The surprising part is that the content itself looks like creator content. Reading through the brand's own 70 posts, more than eight in ten are filmed as single-person, phone-first UGC, the exact format the category pays creators to produce.

The audience the content reaches for is consistent: health-conscious Gen Z and Millennial women chasing convenient wellness (about 55% of the personas the posts target), then busy Type-A professionals worried about digestion and energy, then internet-culture natives who reward self-deprecating humour. It is a coherent voice. What it lacks is anyone outside the brand carrying it.

The Dormant Engine

Rizz already produces creator-style content and has earned real mentions before, the 59.7K-view testimonial being the clearest, yet across the 69-day window not a single creator tagged the brand. The voice exists; the distribution does not.

Start with scale. Across the niche there are 4,699 active ad creatives from 77 advertisers, and only about 11% of all creatives survive past 60 days, in line with the wider industry. The interesting question is what makes that 11%. Among the longest-running creatives, creator-shot UGC is the single most common style at 42%, over-indexing against its 38% share of the full set, and video over-indexes too. The ads that last in this category are filmed by people, not studios.

The same pattern shows up in specific clusters. Four unrelated brands, UGC for paid ads being the common thread, run single-creator testimonial ads that open on a curiosity gap or a price-error hook, each surviving past 60 days. Organic India has one such UGC creative running for 968 days. Theweem keeps three past 430 days. This is durable, compounding creative, not a one-off.

Who is commissioning at volume? Forty-two advertisers run 10 or more live creatives with most refreshed inside the last 30 days, JSHealth at 356 active, Gruns at 334, Goli at 229, several at a 100% fresh-creative pace. They are testing at a cadence only a creator pipeline can feed.

Where does Rizz sit? It has tested 12 ad creatives, and the instinct is right, eight of the twelve were creator-shot UGC. But every one expired inside 10 days and none is live now. The idea is correct; the volume and the persistence are not there yet.

The Commissioning Gap

42 advertisers in the category are each running 10+ live creatives with the majority refreshed in the last 30 days. Rizz has tested 12 and currently runs none. The gap is not budget; it is a creator pipeline producing enough variants to test at category cadence.

Across the 69-day brand window Rizz published 70 posts, split 49 videos, 8 carousels and 13 photos or photo slideshows, at about 7 a week. The average post draws 14 likes and the typical post just 3, numbers that read as a small account still finding reach. Underneath those averages, though, there is a clear signal about what the audience actually rewards.

By content pillar, the brand's relatable wellness humour is both the most published (29 posts) and a strong performer at 21 average likes. Its aspirational glow-up content does slightly better per post at 25 likes across 7 posts. The drag is direct product promotion: 17 posts, nearly a quarter of the feed, averaging just 6 likes. The lowest-performing material is also among the most published, and the format split tells the same story even more sharply.

All five of the top posts are video, and across the whole window video averages 18 likes against 3 for carousels and photo slideshows. The pattern is the familiar one for early brands: the lighter, more personal, phone-first formats outrun the polished static ones. The top post at 129 likes is roughly 9 times the typical post in the dominant humour pillar.

The instruction the data is giving the content team is simple: make more video, make less static, and move promotion out of straight product posts and into the relatable and aspirational formats that already work.

The Biggest Miss

Your videos average 18 likes; your static posts, the carousels and photo slideshows, average 3, roughly 6 times fewer. Yet 30% of what you post is still static. Shift that mix toward video and the same effort buys far more reach.

07 · The opportunity

Six moves that change the game for Rizz.

Opportunity 01

Stand up an always-on creator bench

Rizz earned zero Creator Tagged Posts in the 69-day window while Lemme compounded over 200 on Instagram alone. No brand in this dataset concentrates creators into retainers, so a focused bench is an open lane.

Opportunity 02

Commission UGC built for paid, then let it run

Six brands in this dataset feed their own grid with commissioned UGC; Rizz fills it in-house and tops out near 7 likes a post. Video already outperforms Rizz's static posts about 6 times, so creator video is the obvious next input.
