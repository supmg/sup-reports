Slate sits well outside the proffee pack on the metric that matters for creator marketing: earned advocacy. Over the 181-day niche window, 49 creators posted 51 Creator Tagged Posts tagging Slate for 1.08M creator views, more than Super Coffee, Chike, PRÉS and Throne Sport Coffee combined, and a top-15 finish among all 97 brands in the category despite a mid-tier follower count. That engine is powered by a city-by-city local-creator programme and a standing ambassador roster, and it already feeds 7 live creator-shot UGC ads, the exact format the category's longest-running paid creatives reward. The white space is concentration and platform: no brand in the category runs a retainer bench, and Slate's earned engine barely reaches TikTok, where the category's biggest creator views actually happen.

Where Slate Sits

The follower count is mid-table; the creator footprint is elite. Slate ranks 12 of 97 on Creator Tagged Posts and 15 of 97 on Total Creator Views, ahead of every Protein Coffee and Proffee rival combined, and that gap between audience size and creator pull is what the rest of this report works through.

Across the 181-day brand window, 49 creators posted 55 Creator Tagged Posts tagging Slate, and most of them ran through one repeatable motion: a city-by-city local-creator programme on the Hummingbirds network, each post tied to a nearby Target, Walmart or Costco. This is a deliberate gifting and seeding programme stacked on geo-clusters, and it scales an earned-mention engine that already leads the Protein Coffee and Proffee set.

Because the calibration here is strong, 49 unique creators tagging in the window, the right way to read the paid versus organic split is amplification, not absence. Slate is already generating the exact creator-style content the rest of the category pays to manufacture, and 23 of the 55 posts carry no partnership tag at all, genuine earned mentions around retail launches. The question this raises is why so little of that advocacy reaches TikTok, where the category's biggest creator views happen.

Across the 181-day brand window Slate published 64 posts on its own channels, 9 on Instagram and 55 on TikTok, and the typical one drew just 22 likes. The owned feed is a volume play, heavy on TikTok meme and user-generated reposts, and most of it earns very little. The brand's creators out-perform the brand's own feed many times over, which is the central tension of this report.

By format the signal is sharp. Slate's four Instagram carousels average 9,780 likes while its 54 videos average 497, so the most-published format is also the weakest by a wide margin. By pillar, the high-volume Relatable and Meme content averages 29 likes against 2,939 for the rarer Inspirational posts, a 100-fold gap. The feed is starved of the formats and pillars that actually work on it.

Six moves that compound Slate's creator lead.

Opportunity 01

Build a TikTok creator squad to mirror the Instagram earned engine

Slate has 51 Creator Tagged Posts on Instagram but only 4 on TikTok, despite a 18.9K-follower TikTok feed. The category's highest-reach creator content in the window is on TikTok, where Slate is effectively absent. A standing TikTok creator squad would extend the proven Instagram earned-mention engine onto the platform driving the category's biggest views.

Convert repeat performers into a retainer bench

No brand in the category concentrates creators into a retainer, Slate's own ratio is 1.06 Creator Tagged Posts per creator. Repeat advocates like Derek Pratt already post Slate organically and unprompted across multiple months. A small retainer bench would compound an already-working earned signal rather than fix an absence.
