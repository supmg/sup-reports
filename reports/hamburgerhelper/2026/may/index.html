The category's survival curve is unusual. Of the top 100 creatives in the category map, 21 have run more than 60 days - roughly 28%, against an industry baseline of about 11%. In that 60-plus-day pool, UGC outweighs every other style: 24% are creator-shot, against the 42% creator-shot share across all active creatives. Creator content survives at higher rates than studio content, in this category, by a large margin.

The brands feeding that pool aren't testing one or two creator videos. They're cycling many. Four niche brands have 10 or more live creatives with the majority cycled in under 30 days - Ramen Bae, The Only Bean, Once Upon a Farm, and Land O'Lakes Mac & Cheese all show the high-volume, high-churn pattern of a creator pipeline producing fresh variants every week. The 8 UGC ads surviving 60+ days didn't appear on the first try; they emerged from a testing pool ten times their size.

Across the 80-day brand window, Hamburger Helper published 13 own-feed posts on Instagram. The format split skews to carousels (1), with a smaller number of videos and a single photo. Average likes per post sit at 121; median at 50. Underneath those averages, though, the per-pillar numbers tell a much sharper story.

The Promotional posts (3 across the window) average 384 likes. The Relatable / Meme posts (10 across the window) average 42. The gap is 9.1×. The Promotional posts include the Helper Deluxe Breakfast Hashbrowns giveaway (660 likes, 2,760 comments - a giveaway mechanic), a Helper Deluxe launch teaser (413 likes), and a Helper Deluxe new-line announcement (81 likes). The Relatable / Meme posts include a Masters Champions Dinner satire (58 likes), a side-by-side "wordy ad vs meme ad" comparison (84 likes), and various Lefty-mascot meme drops in the 3 to 50 like range. The pattern is clear: the audience shows up for product launches and giveaway mechanics, and the brand publishes one of those for every seven memes.
